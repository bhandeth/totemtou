The Cold Desert

Before the days,
the place.

Upper Dolpo sits in the northwest of Nepal, against the Tibetan border. Geographically and culturally it is part of the Tibetan plateau, not the Nepali mid-hills. It rains here only in the Indian-summer fringes; the rest of the year it is wind, light, and rock.

This is the longest journey we run, and the only one we run by bike. Read this section before the days. The days will read differently afterwards.

i.

A Tibetan plateau the map calls Nepal

Dolpo was part of the ancient Ngari region of western Tibet. The early settlers practised Bön, the indigenous belief system that pre-dates Buddhism on the plateau. From the eleventh century onward, Tibetan Buddhism took root alongside it. The two have lived together here ever since — in the same villages, sometimes in the same family.

The land sits in the rain shadow of the Dhaulagiri massif. Almost no monsoon reaches it. The villages farm barley and graze yak; the trade that built them ran north into Tibet, not south into Nepal. Walking through Dolpo is, in most practical senses, walking through a country that is no longer on the map.

ii.

The salt road, before the road

For centuries, Dolpo was a salt corridor. Yak caravans carried salt from the Tibetan plateau over the high passes into Dolpo, and traded it south for grain, butter, and cloth. The same passes we cross — Niwar La, Mola, Sela La, Nagdalo La — were the working passes of that trade.

The road still ends well short of Upper Dolpo. Past Sangda, there is no road, and there will not be a road for some time. Trails the salt caravans used are still the trails the local boys ride their Chinese motocross bikes on. We ride and push our bikes along the same thread.

iii.

Shey Gompa, the spiritual heart

At around 4,400 m, sitting beneath Crystal Mountain, Shey Gompa was built in the eleventh century and is the spiritual centre of Dolpo. The monastery is the destination of a once-every-twelve-years pilgrimage that circles the mountain. We sleep one of the trip's nights at Shey, and rest the next day there.

Two monasteries on this journey are not stops on the way to somewhere — they are the somewhere. Namgung is the other. The day spent inside each is one of the reasons the route is twenty days and not fifteen.

iv.

Phoksundo, the lake

Shey Phoksundo is the deepest lake in Nepal — 145 m at its centre, sitting at 3,611 m, ringed by cliffs and a dry blue that doesn't quite look like other water. The Tibetan name for the village beside it, Ringmo, means 'long' for the shape of the lake. We descend to it from the Nagdalo La on the seventeenth day. The trail down is long, exposed, and one of the more spectacular descents in the Himalaya.

v.

What riding a bike here actually means

Roughly ten percent of this expedition is rideable trail. Another forty percent is bike on shoulder, walking up. The remainder is jeep transfer in and out, and short flying segments. We say "MTB Expedition" carefully — this is not a cycling holiday. The bike is the right tool for the descents, for the rideable valleys near Tinje and Khomas, and for the long valley out of Phoksundo to Dunai. For the passes, the bike is a load you carry over the mountain.

We tell every applicant this directly on the call. Riders who have done long-format expeditions already understand it. Riders who haven't sometimes need to hear it twice.

The Days

Twenty days, walked, ridden, and pushed.

Twenty days is too long to read as a list. We've grouped the route into seven phases — each one a different kind of day, with a different relationship to the bike. The frame above runs underneath all of them.

iPokhara · prep · drive in

The bikes are built. The first warm-up rides happen here.

Days1–3 Modejeep, short rides Sleephotel, then teahouse
  • Day 1Arrive Pokhara · final gear check, bike assembly, test ride toward Sarangkot or Peace Pagoda.822 m
  • Day 2Warm-up loop · Pokhara valley — Kaskikot, Pame, Begnas. Last day at low altitude.822 m
  • Day 3Long drive to Thini · 8–9 hours up the Kali Gandaki gorge into Lower Mustang.2,700 m

Two days in Pokhara is not a layover. The bikes are unboxed, built, and tuned at the same workshop the founders use; cleat positions are checked, brake pads bedded in, tubeless sealant topped up. The first ride is a short loop to Sarangkot or the Peace Pagoda — enough to find anything wrong with the build before the road heads north.

Day three is the long drive. Pokhara to Thini is eight or nine hours through the Kali Gandaki, a gorge that runs between Annapurna and Dhaulagiri and is, by the standard measure of river to ridgeline, the deepest in the world. The road climbs from sub-tropical green into high-desert brown over the course of one afternoon. The body needs the time. Thini is a quiet, working settlement at 2,700 m, far enough off the main Mustang trail to feel local rather than touristed.

Place — Thini

One of Mustang's oldest villages

Thini sits a short distance from Jomsom but feels older. Stone houses, barley fields, prayer wheels turned by elderly villagers in the same way they've been turned for generations. We stop here on purpose: most groups push straight to Jomsom or Kagbeni; we sleep where the day actually wants to end.

iiMustang · entry to Dolpo

From Thini, into Lower Mustang. Then the road runs out.

Days4–5 Moderide · jeep · push Sleepteahouse, then homestay/camp
  • Day 4Thini–Muktinath–Lupra–Thini · loop ride for acclimatisation, no luggage.3,760 m
  • Day 5Thini → Sangda La (4,990 m) → Sangda · jeep section over high passes; entry to Dolpo proper.4,990 m

Day four is the first full day on the bike at altitude. We ride up to Muktinath at 3,760 m — a temple complex sacred to Hindus and Buddhists, with the eternal-flame shrine of Jwala Mai inside its grounds — then drop into the Lupra valley and back. No luggage on the bikes. The intent is acclimatisation, not distance.

Day five is the entry to Dolpo. We take a jeep over the Sangda La at 4,990 m. It is the first serious altitude of the trip, and there is a reason it is in the jeep and not on the bike: the air at 4,990 m, on day five, asks too much. The road ends shortly after. From here on, we are on bikes or on foot. Sangda is the last settlement that has, in any meaningful way, a connection to the outside.

Place — Sangda La

The threshold

Crossing the Sangda La is the trip's threshold. South of it is Mustang — a region with electricity, intermittent network, jeep-roads. North of it is Upper Dolpo, where almost none of those things exist past the next village. The pass is also where, in the historical sense, Nepal becomes Tibet.

iiiNiwar La · Mola · Tinje

Two passes in three days. The hardest section of the trip.

Days6–8 Passes5,125 m · 5,030 m Sleepcamp, homestay, camp
  • Day 6Sangda → Niwar La Base Camp · ride, push, carry. First real wilderness camp.4,800 m
  • Day 7Cross Niwar La (5,125 m) → Chharka Bhot · long technical descent off the pass.4,302 m
  • Day 8Chharka Bhot → Mola Pass (5,030 m) → Tinje · long crossing day, beautiful descent.4,150 m

This is the section that defines the trip's grade. Three days, two passes above 5,000 m, one wilderness camp on the way to the first one. The bike is on your shoulder for hours at a time; the gradient out of Sangda toward Niwar La is too steep and too high to ride. The compensations come later — the descent off the Niwar La into the Chharka valley is long enough that the morning's climb will, for a while, feel worth it.

Whenever we thought today was the easy day, it turned out to be a killing kind of a day. — Past rider, Dolpo MTB

Chharka Bhot is one of the more isolated villages on the route. There are a handful of homestays, no shop in any sense you would recognise, and dal bhat for dinner. The morning of day eight starts early; the climb to the Mola is gradual but long, and the descent on the far side, into Tinje, is the first real flowing trail of the trip. After three days of carrying the bike, the act of riding it is briefly extraordinary.

Place — Niwar La

5,125 m, on a bike day

The pass is a saddle of grey rock with prayer flags. The wind is steady. There is no view that isn't of more mountain. The air, by the partial-pressure measure, is at fifty-three percent of sea level. Most groups stop here for fifteen minutes, no more — long enough to catch breath, drink water, and start the descent before the cold gets in.

ivTinje · Shimen · Khomas · Saldang

Where the trip is rideable. Hidden valleys, ancient villages, the longest single-trails.

Days9–12 Moderide · explore · rest Sleephomestays
  • Day 9Rest and exploration · Tinje village.4,150 m
  • Day 10Tinje → Shimen → Khomas · flowing valley ride through hidden Dolpo settlements.4,200 m
  • Day 11Rest and exploration · Khomas village.4,200 m
  • Day 12Khomas → Saldang → Namgung · short technical day; ancient monastery in the evening.4,400 m

After the crossings, the route opens. The valleys between Tinje and Saldang sit at around 4,100 to 4,400 metres — high enough that the air still asks something of you, low enough that the bike works again. Most of these days are rideable. The trail comes in long, flowing single-track sections through arid red and grey landscape, with occasional crossings of small settlements where children stop to look.

We build in two rest days here on purpose. Tinje and Khomas are the kind of villages where staying a day matters — there are gompas, traditional homes, fields being worked, people who will sit and talk through the guide's translation. Without the rest, the next phase is too much to ask of the body. With it, the next phase is a pilgrimage.

Saldang is the largest settlement in this part of Upper Dolpo — culturally rich, with several monasteries and a proper Tibetan-plateau feel. Namgung Gompa, where we sleep, is one of the older monasteries on the route. The day ends short specifically so the evening at the gompa is long.

Place — Saldang

The largest village in the deep Dolpo

Saldang sits in a wide upland basin, with terraces of barley and houses in the Tibetan style. It was an important node on the salt trade. The cultural texture is intact — a rare thing in the Himalaya in 2026 — because the road has not yet reached it. Whether that holds is a question every visitor is, indirectly, part of the answer to.

Field notes
"We passed through only a few villages, but everywhere we were welcomed with great warmth and openness. Our guide translated for us, or we communicated with hands and feet. Eventually the road ended completely, leaving only simple trails. The landscape became even more remote, even emptier."

vSela La · Shey · Nagdalo La

The spiritual heart, then the highest point.

Days13–15 Passes5,090 m · 5,350 m Sleepcamp at Shey, then homestay at Phoksundo
  • Day 13Namgung → Sela La (5,090 m) → Shey Gompa · cross, then a long descending single-track to Shey.4,400 m
  • Day 14Rest day · Shey Gompa, Crystal Mountain.4,400 m
  • Day 15Shey → Nagdalo La (5,350 m) → Ringmo (Phoksundo) · the highest pass, then the long descent to the lake.3,611 m

Day thirteen is a working day for the body and a still day for everything else. The Sela La sits at 5,090 m; the trail to it is rideable in places and a push in others. The descent on the far side, into the basin where Shey sits beneath Crystal Mountain, is one of the longest single-track sections of the whole trip. By late afternoon you are at the gompa.

Day fourteen is rest. Shey Gompa is the spiritual centre of Upper Dolpo, built in the eleventh century, and the destination of a pilgrimage that circles Crystal Mountain once every twelve years. The day is short on activity. Walking around the kora, sitting in the gompa, drinking butter tea with a monk — the trip slows here on purpose.

Day fifteen is the high day. The Nagdalo La at 5,350 m is the highest point of the journey and the last serious crossing. The push up is long and exposed; the descent on the far side is a fourteen-kilometre drop on technical trail toward Phoksundo Lake. The trail is steep, shaped by yak hooves over centuries, and rideable in long sections that you will, for the rest of your life, remember in detail.

Place — Shey Gompa

The eleventh-century monastery

Shey Gompa was made famous in Peter Matthiessen's The Snow Leopard, the 1978 book that brought the region to a Western readership for the first time. The gompa is still active. The monks live here year-round, snowed in for parts of the winter. We arrive in their season; we keep our voices low, we leave the place as we found it.

Place — Nagdalo La

5,350 m, on a bike, on day fifteen

By day fifteen, the body has been at altitude long enough that the Nagdalo is harder than its number suggests. The pass is technical and exposed; the wind on the saddle is steady and cold even in spring. We start early. We give ourselves all day. The descent toward Phoksundo is, for many riders, the single most spectacular descent of any trip we run.

Field notes
"Towards the end, we spent two stages in Buddhist monasteries. These places gave us a deep and fascinating insight into the local culture and spirituality. The final pass — over 5,300 metres — was a special moment. One last look back before beginning our return."
Manuel  ·  past rider  ·  2024
viPhoksundo · Dunai

The lake, then the long ride out.

Days16–17 Modewalk and rest, then long descent Sleephomestay, then lodge
  • Day 16Rest at Phoksundo · short rides to viewpoints, walks along the shore.3,611 m
  • Day 17Phoksundo → Suligad → Dunai · long technical descent on river trails.2,140 m

Day sixteen is a true rest day. Phoksundo is the deepest lake in Nepal — 145 m at its centre, sitting at 3,611 m, ringed by cliffs and a colour of blue that doesn't quite read on the eye. There are short rides up to viewpoints. There are slow walks along the shore. The body is recovering from fifteen days of work; the head is starting to register what has happened.

Day seventeen is one of the longest descents in the Himalaya: from Phoksundo down to Dunai is a drop of nearly 1,500 metres on technical river trail, with several gorge sections that are rideable in skilled hands and walkable for everyone else. By late afternoon, you are in Dunai — a real town, with electricity, with a shop, with restaurants. The body and the head, between them, register that they are out.

Place — Phoksundo

The deepest lake in Nepal

Phoksundo holds a colour that local lore attributes to a mineral content unique to its catchment. The village of Ringmo, at one end, was built before the lake had a road or a permit system — it was a stop on the salt trade. Today, the lake is part of Shey Phoksundo National Park, and one of the more protected high-altitude ecosystems in the Himalaya.

Field notes
"When we reached Dunai, we felt like now we are alive — and we took a whole day there just to eat food."
Deepak  ·  founder · scouting ride  ·  2023
viiNepalgunj · Kathmandu

From the high plateau to the lowlands. Then a flight east, and home.

Days18–20 Modejeep · domestic flight Sleephotel
  • Day 18Dunai → Nepalgunj · early jeep down through the lower hills to the Terai.150 m
  • Day 19Nepalgunj → Kathmandu · morning flight east, rest day, gear sort.1,400 m
  • Day 20Kathmandu · departure, or extension into a wider Nepal trip.1,400 m

The exit is the inverse of the entry. From Dunai down to Nepalgunj on the Terai plain is a long jeep, an early start, and a profound shift of climate — from cold high desert to humid jungle in a single day. By evening you are in a hotel with running hot water, which after twenty days takes a few minutes to register.

The flight from Nepalgunj to Kathmandu is a morning hop. The capital, after Dolpo, feels like an entirely different country, which in many practical senses it is. The last full day is for sleeping, sorting gear, walking around the Boudha or Patan, and saying the kind of goodbye that people who have spent twenty days at altitude together say to each other.

Place — Nepalgunj & the Terai

A different country, on the same map

The Terai is the strip of low-lying jungle that runs along Nepal's southern border. Wildlife, big rivers, sub-tropical heat. We pass through it briefly. For riders extending the trip, we can route into Bardia or Chitwan from Nepalgunj — a separate journey, with separate logistics, that sits well after Dolpo.

Field notes

Is this for you?

The honest answer, at the top of the scale.

We grade our journeys on a 1–6 scale. Upper Dolpo sits at six. There is nothing harder on our slate. Almost everyone who applies is told some version of: not yet.

Level 6 — ExtremeTwenty days, four passes above 5,000 m, MTB-handling at altitude 1 → 6

This is the hardest journey on our slate. The grade is not marketing language — twenty days at altitude, four passes over 5,000 m, and a bike that you carry as much as you ride is, by any reasonable measure, an expedition. Riders join this trip with prior multi-day MTB experience and prior altitude experience, separately. If you have one but not the other, the right answer is to walk a high journey first, ride a long one second, and apply for Dolpo third.

We are honest with applicants on the call. The scouting ride that informed this itinerary was harder than the founders had planned for; we built the route as it now stands so that future cohorts walk into the difficulty with their eyes open, and with rest days, contingencies, and a medical kit designed for what the actual terrain can do.

If the conversation suggests Dolpo is one trip too soon, we'll say so. Most applicants we say it to come back two seasons later, ready.

If you're ready for this, you have already —
  • Completed at least one multi-day MTB expedition of 7+ days
  • Spent at least one continuous week above 4,000 m before
  • Carried and pushed a loaded bike on technical singletrack
  • Twelve weeks out, with time to train specifically for this
  • Ridden tubeless and managed your own basic mechanicals
  • Slept in a tent at altitude, in cold, more than once

What we carry, and what we plan for.

Logistics  ·  Safety

The medical & mechanical kit on the trail

The kit is sized for the route. Twenty days, four passes over 5,000 m, and most of the route a day or more from the nearest road means we carry more than the smaller treks need. Mechanicals matter as much as medical: a broken derailleur on day eleven is a day-long problem if you don't have the spare. The kit is carried by the lead guide and the support crew, and re-stocked between expeditions.

  • Portable oxygen — multiple cylinders
  • Pulse oximeter & BP monitor
  • Stethoscope
  • AMS protocol kit · Diamox, Dexamethasone
  • HACE / HAPE response kit
  • Trauma kit · splints, dressings, sutures
  • Folding stretcher
  • Spare derailleur hangers
  • Spare brake pads, chains, sealant
  • Bike multi-tools, chain breaker
  • Hand-warmers, emergency bivy bags
  • Satellite communicator · two units

If something doesn't go to plan

Five of the failure modes we plan for, and how the day works when they happen.

  • Altitude affects a member of the group We have built rest days into days 9, 11, 14 and 16. If a rider needs to descend, we descend; the group stays at the last village while the rider and a guide drop to lower altitude with the satellite communicator open. Dolpo is twenty days because the rest days exist, not in spite of them.
  • A pass is closed by snow or weather On Dolpo, the four 5,000 m passes are weather-dependent in shoulder season. We hold a day at the last settlement before the pass and reassess; if the window doesn't open, we have alternative routes via lower (slower) passes — pre-walked by the lead — that get us to Shey or Phoksundo without crossing the Niwar or Nagdalo at full height.
  • A serious mechanical failure on a bike We carry spare derailleur hangers, brake pads, chains, sealant, and a full multi-tool. For frame or wheel damage that the kit cannot fix, the rider continues by yak or by foot to the next village, and a replacement bike is moved up from Dunai or Pokhara to meet the group at Phoksundo or earlier. Local boys riding Chinese motocross bikes have, more than once, helped extract a broken bike from a distant valley.
  • Weight limit on the domestic flights Flights from Nepalgunj and Juphal cap at 15–20 kg including hand baggage. We pre-stage non-essential luggage in Pokhara and Kathmandu; what the rider carries on the trail is genuinely what they need.
  • Evacuation from above 4,000 m The satellite communicators carried by the lead and the support crew connect to Nepali helicopter evacuation services. We have the registered policies and the contact tree pre-loaded. Travel insurance covering MTB at high altitude is a non-negotiable condition of joining this trip.
